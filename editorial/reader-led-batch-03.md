## Medical claim review

- A15: oral iron can cause nausea, constipation and abdominal discomfort. A treatment
  plan can be reviewed with the prescriber/pharmacist; no universal dose or change
  in timing is prescribed. Severe/concerning symptoms require prompt advice.
  Source: https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/ferrous-sulfate/side-effects-of-ferrous-sulfate/
  Timing/tolerability context: https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/ferrous-sulfate/about-ferrous-sulfate/
- D15: ferritin is an iron-storage protein and blood concentrations may rise with
  inflammation/infection. Results need clinical context and may be interpreted
  with other measures; no universal target or diagnostic threshold is offered.
  Source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/
- E16: iron may interact with medications. The post requests an individual pharmacist
  review rather than inventing one spacing interval for all products.
  Source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/
- A16 and E15 concern describing experience, not attributing recovery to a specific
  intervention. D16 concerns evidence interpretation and does not assert that iron
  treats or replaces ADHD treatment.
